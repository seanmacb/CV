\noindent
I am applying to this fellowship because I intend to build a career like Leon Lederman’s, one in which pushing the frontiers of physics and sharing it with the public are equally important commitments.
My own path into physics began at a winter astronomy camp in Northeast Vermont, and every outreach activity I have taken on since - from public observatory nights at my undergraduate college to Q\&A sessions at city-wide museum events - has been an attempt to ignite a similar spark in someone else. 
As a Lederman Fellow, I will share that passion with the Chicagoland community, using the Lederman Science Center, Fermilab's Office of Education and Public Engagement, and the wider Chicagoland science network as a platform to communicate and educate both children and adults, about general principles of physics, dark energy, and gravitational-waves.

\statement{Past Outreach, Teaching, and Mentoring Experience}
% \TODO{Intro statement}
% Throughout my career, I have passionately pursued outreach, teaching, and mentoring opportunities, starting as an observatory guide as an undergraduate student in 2016, and extending to public science nights in late-summer of this year.
%% OUTREACH
As an undergraduate at Wheaton College (2016-2020), I led public observatory nights every Friday, helping visitors of all backgrounds find and understand objects in the night sky using small 6'' telescopes. This was my first experience translating a technical instrument into a shared, human experience, and was a seminal event that solidified my interest and desire to engage with and inform the public through science. 
% \textbf{Mention CUWip?}
As a graduate student at \ac{um}, I worked with the \ac{um} Community of Physicists for Inclusion and Equity to lead public demonstrations with elementary and middle school students at Metro Detroit Youth Day in 2023, including magnetic induction and the classical rotational inertia experiment on an office chair.
During the 2023 annular and 2024 total solar eclipses, I took advantage of the cosmic coincidence to lead a group of Vermont high-school students from my hometown through lectures and demonstrations about the physics of the eclipses, eclipse safety, and ultimately observe of the eclipse from the path of totality. 
In 2025, I helped to guide visitors at Rubin Observatory as we commissioned the LSST Camera, translating technical knowledge to audiences ranging from political dignitaries, NSF and DOE review teams, and friends and family groups. In addition to providing tours, I gave interviews to the BBC, the Wall Street Journal, and Science News about LSST-Camera, and commissioning the observatory.
Most recently, in September 2026, I led an \textit{Ask a Scientist} session on cosmology and gravitational waves at the \ac{uzh}'s Science Pavilion for the City of Zurich's Long Night of Museums, an annual event in which the city's science, art, and culture museums open their doors free of charge.
Across these activities, the opportunity to connect with the public through science has left me with a deep sense of fulfillment, with each occasion providing the passion to fuel the next. While each experience has been unique, a common thread has been the importance of being affable, sincere, and receptive to individuals of all ages and backgrounds.

%% TEACHING

Along with outreach, I have taught physics courses across a range of institutional settings, each of which has shaped how I think about connecting with students. 
At Wheaton College, a small liberal arts institution, I assisted in teaching introductory physics courses in classes of $\sim25$ students, where the small class sizes allowed me to tailor instruction to individual students and mentor them directly through laboratory-based instruction and exercises. 
At \ac{um}, I served as a lead graduate student instructor for introductory physics for the life sciences, a large-enrollment course of $\sim1,000$ students spread across $\sim20$ different sections. There, I learned to incorporate structure and active learning into large laboratory settings, to help students remain engaged and supported at a larger scale. 
At \ac{uzh}, I taught astroparticle physics for Master’s level students, guiding smaller groups of $\sim10$ students each term toward modern research questions and independent scientific thinking. 
Across these settings, I have experienced firsthand how students with different backgrounds, goals, and ambitions encounter physics, and I will bring that breadth of experience to my outreach and education efforts as a Lederman Fellow.

\statement{My Plan for Outreach as a Lederman Fellow}
As a Lederman Fellow, I want to pair frontier research, including that of my future Fermilab colleagues, with public science communication, building on the Dark Matter Days events, science-trivia nights, and QICK tutorials for the quantum program. I will extend this model to gravitational-wave and multi-messenger cosmology. % My goal is to strike a tricky balance, where visitors can feel curious, yet have the core understanding of the general features of .
% % Outreach at Fermilab
To augment Saturday Morning physics, I would propose and give talks on observational astrophysics, cosmology, and new gravitational-wave detections. These presentations will increase in relevance as the LSST continues on its ten-year survey, and new analyses reveal more about the universe. In addition to presenting about observational astronomy, I want to play a key role in helping the wider public become more familiar with gravitational waves, especially as new discoveries are made, including the next bright siren.

In addition to Saturday Morning physics, I will work to bring \textit{Ask a Scientist}-like sessions to Fermilab. In these sessions, I would help connect my own research expertise, but also incorporate other experts at Fermilab to emphasize the strong collider, quantum, neutrino, theory, and dark matter programs. The event would follow a similar structure to Dark Matter Days, but instead of showcasing different experiments and concepts in one research area, the event would have different themes for each session, with each session featuring a Q\&A period and interactive demonstrations.

% Outreach in the Chicagoland area
I am eager to bring my outreach activities to the wider Chicagoland area, expanding beyond the Fermilab grounds. Chicago hosts a rich ecosystem of public science venues, and I would aim to connect Fermilab's physics with audiences that may never visit the laboratory itself. At the Adler Planetarium, I would participate in Astronomy Conversations, a long-running collaboration between Adler and the University of Chicago, in which researchers speak directly with museum visitors of all ages about their work, using the Space Visualization Laboratory to ground the discussion in interactive demonstrations. 
% I would extend this approach to the Museum of Science and Industry, whose broad, family-oriented audience is well suited to bringing particle physics and cosmology to a general public. 
To connect with adult audiences, I would join the Chicago chapter of Astronomy on Tap, collaborating with the Northwestern University graduate students who manage the program, to help lower the barrier to asking questions in an informal setting and reach people outside the usual science-museum crowd.

In addition to recurring outreach activities in Chicago and at Fermilab, I aim to leave a legacy at the Lederman Science center by building a gravitational wave module to help visualize the propagation of gravitational waves. This kind of visualization will help members of the public experience gravitational waves using one of the best senses to connect with a scientific concept --- their eyes.
While this module would serve as a permanent fixture in the Lederman Science center, I will work to build a similar module that can be used in QuarkNet teacher workshops, helping to add a new exhibition for another fundamental force.

% % MENTORING - around Chicago and at Fermilab
Building on these outreach programs, I will serve as an active and engaged mentor for undergraduate and high school students at Fermilab. In my career, active mentors have had an immensely positive influence on my own trajectory. 
To pay it forward, 
I will volunteer as a judge and project mentor for the Illinois Junior Academy of Science's regional expositions and the CPS Student STEM Exhibition \& Symposium. 
In addition to mentoring groups of junior students, I will support students through Fermilab's TARGET high-school internship program, and the SULI and CCI undergraduate internship program, extending the same one-on-one research mentorship that has guided me to a variety of rewarding opportunities.

% Taken together, these are not activities I would bolt onto the Lederman Fellowship as an afterthought. 
As each outreach opportunity before has fueled the next one, I am eager to reestablish this cycle in the Chicagoland area as a Lederman fellow.
Each of these projects are a continuation of what I have already been doing at every stage of my career, 
now with the platform, the institutional history, and the surrounding science ecosystem to turn this plan into reality. 
I would be proud to carry that piece of Leon Lederman's legacy forward as a fellow.
