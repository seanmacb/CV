\textbf{Instrumentation Development for the Cosmic Frontier:}
I will continue in a support-scientist role for LSSTCam as the camera transitions from early operations into sustained \LSST\ survey operations, maintaining the operational knowledge needed to respond quickly to sensor anomalies and focal-plane issues as they arise.
Building on my commissioning-era characterization of focal-plane defects, I will deploy a defect-monitoring tool, currently in-development, to \texttt{cp\_verify}, the calibration-product verification framework used by the \LSST\ Science Pipelines. Bringing this tool from isolated development to a published resource will move defect monitoring from a manual to automated process, directly improving the quality of \LSST\ data.

The central bottleneck for transient astrophysics and standard siren cosmology in the \LSST\ era is not imaging depth, but spectroscopic capacity: both dark and bright siren measurements ultimately require secure redshifts for host galaxies and rapid characterization of transients from \LSST's alert stream. To aid this effort, I will expand on my expertise from testing the robotic positioners of \DESI\ to test advanced focal plane technology for future multi-object spectrographs.
As part of this focus, I will explore instrument design for a multi-object spectrograph optimized for standard siren cosmology, specialized for the precision and event rates of forthcoming gravitational wave detectors. This instrument would serve two goals; 1) characterize astrophysical transients associated with potential bright standard sirens, and 2) obtain spectroscopic redshifts from host galaxies to bolster dark siren measurements. Through this dual purpose, the proposed instrument would become a dark energy science machine primed to capitalize on the deluge of data from \LSST\ and future GW observatories. % MOS instrumentation development
These pathfinding activities would help to inform the proposed \specsfive, a future, large-scale cosmic frontier experiment. Development and testing of robotic positioners for the focal plane will help shape instrument capabilities so it serves as an effective spectroscopic complement to \Rubin, starting in the mid-2030's. % Spec-S5 instrumentation development.
