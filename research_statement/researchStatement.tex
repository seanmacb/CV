\bigskip

My research focuses on exploring the cosmic frontier using electromagnetic and gravitational wave data to constrain cosmology.
This focus is underscored by a research program with three distinct pillars: building cutting-edge astronomical instrumentation, leading observing campaigns to search for the electromagnetic counterparts to gravitational-wave mergers, and performing inference on electromagnetic and gravitational-wave data to achieve robust cosmological constraints.
These three pillars reflect the approach I aim to bring to my next postdoctoral position at \Institute, where each research activity is not viewed as separate, but as three legs of one research program, strongest when pursued together. 
Here, I describe my past work in support of the three pillars of my research program, and my plans to adapt this program for the future.

\statement{Astronomical Instrumentation}
I believe that trustworthy astrophysical measurements begin with trustworthy astronomical instruments, which is why I have spent as much time building and characterizing hardware as I have spent on cutting-edge analysis. My instrumentation work has encompassed both photometric and spectroscopic instruments, with a specific focus on focal plane technology. % Opener

This began with \LLAMAS, an integral field spectrograph for the Magellan Baade telescope. I validated the optical performance of the diffraction gratings and collimators for the \LLAMAS\ spectrographs, assembled and verified each of the 24 spectrograph camera optical assemblies, and integrated all 2,400 AR-coated fibers into the focal plane with zero fiber loss \citep{Simcoe2022LLAMAS}. \LLAMAS\ was commissioned in 2024, and is currently providing high fidelity spectroscopic data in the southern hemisphere \cite{llamas}. % LLAMAS
After integrating and testing \LLAMAS, I shifted from integral-field spectrographs to multi-object spectrographs, retaining a focus on focal plane systems. While at the University of Michigan-Ann Arbor, I characterized and resolved a common failure mode on \DESI\ robotic positioners, known as the linear phi failure mode. While testing the \DESI\ positioners, I found that reorienting the robotic positioner to an inverted physical configuration could remove the linear phi behavior, indicating that gravity-sag has a significant influence on the failure mode \citep{Wuthrich2026Positioners}.  Recently, I have been testing robotic fiber positioners for next-generation spectroscopic focal planes, including the \must\ and the proposed \specsfive, and am currently leading fiber tilt-sensitivity tests of prototype positioners, including both static and dynamic measurements on a kinematic telescope mount. % Robotic positioners

This expertise with spectroscopic instruments translated effectively to the transcendent photometric imager of the next decade, the \LSSTCam. Since 2024, I have served as an \LSSTCam\ Summit Support Scientist, leading the \LSSTCam's focal-plane commissioning on Cerro Pach\'on as a key component of the in-kind contribution from Switzerland to the Rubin Project. I tested the CCDs in both the summit clean-room, and on the Simonyi Survey Telescope, optimizing the CCD performance, including removal of a dominant instrumental signature in \LSSTCam\ images known as 'persistence'. This improvement, among other optimizations, is detailed in the invited proceedings of SPIE Astronomical Telescopes + Instrumentation 2026, the most important astronomical instrumentation conference \citep{MacBride2026LSSTCam}. Additionally, I characterized CCD defects in the \LSSTCam\ focal plane, and developed a custom image-processing tool to monitor defect stability over time. This tool is currently deployed alongside a range of other pipelines to monitor \LSSTCam\ focal plane health over the ten year \LSST\ \TODO{Cite this?}. % LSSTCam

\statement{Standard Siren Cosmology}
While large observing campaigns have not yielded an electromagnetic counterpart to a gravitational wave merger in recent \IGWN\ observing runs, I have retained a focus on constraining cosmology using electromagnetic and gravitational wave data, employing the \textit{dark siren} method to measure the expansion rate of the local universe, \Ho.

This work began by supporting the preparation of \DES\ data, resulting in the first dark siren constraint to use a deep photometric catalog to jointly infer gravitational wave population parameters and cosmological parameters \citep{McMahon2026DESY6H0}. \DES\ was accepted as the fiducial \Ho\ result for the most recent \IGWN\ analysis, and demonstrated a 22\% improvement in constraining \Ho\ \citep{LVK2026GWTC5}. Building on the \DES\ analysis, I am now leading a dark-siren analysis using the \DELVE\ galaxy catalog, where initial results show \DELVE\ outperforms \DES, obtaining an \Ho\ constraint at 11\% precision when combined with the bright siren GW170817 \citep{MacBride2026DELVEH0}. % DES+DELVE

With the imminent arrival of \LSST\ data, I have been forecasting the constraining power of dark sirens using simulated \LSST\ data under a range of survey conditions for \DESC. Following the recent release of \LSST\ data from Data Preview 2, I am now leading the first dark siren measurement using \LSST\ data, as a pathfinding measurement ahead of the wide-area data release from Data Release 1. %, supported by a 3M CPU-hour NERSC Science At Scale grant. % LSST DP2

In addition to making standard siren measurements using existing data, I have been assessing how to best improve cosmological constraints through redshift precision alone, with an eye to future specialized surveys. This effort aims to identify the most efficient use of telescope resources to maximize dark siren constraints, using both the spectroscopic observations obtained as part of the \DESGW\ collaboration with \JGEM, and augmenting photometric redshifts using medium band observations from \DECam\ in an accepted observing program where I am a Co-I, leading the image processing. % NORTHSTARS + PRIMO

Beyond Rubin, my spectroscopic follow-up work identifies a concrete capability gap: the southern hemisphere is spectroscopically under-resourced relative to LSST's imaging reach, and transient and host-galaxy redshifts are the scarce resource. I will quantify this need in survey-design terms -- how many redshifts, of which galaxies, at what precision, most improve a dark-siren constraint per hour of telescope time -- and use the answer to motivate a small- or mid-scale southern spectroscopic experiment, a natural vehicle for instrument-development funding and student projects. On the largest scale, I will engage Spec-S5 as the cosmic frontier experiment of the next decade, converting my focal-plane experience from LLAMAS, the DESI linear-phi failure mode, and my ongoing MUST and Spec-S5 positioner tilt-sensitivity measurements into a defined role setting fiber-positioning and target-density requirements.
